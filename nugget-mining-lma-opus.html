Chapter 01 · Orientation

What Are Thinking Artifacts?

When you ask a modern "reasoning" model a hard question, it doesn't just blurt out an answer. First it produces a stream of intermediate text — working notes where it restates the problem, weighs options, checks itself, and sometimes talks itself out of mistakes. The industry calls this a chain of thought (CoT), a reasoning trace, or a thinking artifact. It is the model's scratch paper.

Prospector's analogy. The final answer is the polished nugget in the display case. The thinking artifact is the muddy pan of gravel it came from — where you can actually see what was sifted, what was discarded, and whether the vein looks rich or played out.

The vocabulary a miner needs

Terms you will encounter in the field
TermPlain-English meaning
Chain of Thought (CoT)The step-by-step working text a model generates before its final answer.
Reasoning tokensThe (often billed) units of that thinking text. More thinking = more tokens = more cost and latency.
Raw trace vs. thought summarySome providers show the unfiltered thinking; most now show a summarized, cleaned-up version of it. These are not the same thing.
Thinking / reasoning effortA dial (minimal → high/max) that controls how much a model deliberates before answering.
FaithfulnessHow accurately the visible trace reflects what actually drove the answer. This is the single most important concept in this guide.
Why now Reasoning models moved from novelty to default in 2025–2026. Frontier families from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and DeepSeek all expose some form of thinking artifact, and enterprise observability platforms (Datadog, Dynatrace, Langfuse, Arize) now treat "reasoning steps" as a first-class thing to capture and audit. The artifacts are lying on the ground — the question is how to mine them without getting fooled.

The essential caveat, stated up front

A thinking artifact is a generated narrative, not a log file. Researchers at Arizona State University have argued that treating these "intermediate tokens" as literal thoughts gives "a false sense of model capability and correctness." The text often looks like human scratch work — complete with "hmm," "wait," and "aha" — but that resemblance doesn't prove the model uses it the way you would. Mine it for value, but never mistake the map for the territory.

Chapter 02 · The Assay Office

The Business Case for Mining

Recent research and enterprise practice point to real, daily-use value in thinking artifacts — alongside real costs. Here is the honest ledger.

▲ Potential Benefits

  • Debugging & prompt engineering. The trace shows where a model misread your instructions, so you fix the prompt instead of guessing.
  • Auditability & traceability. In regulated settings, captured reasoning steps form part of an audit trail ("what happened during this decision"), which observability vendors now log by default.
  • Catching errors before they ship. Traces surface a wrong assumption or a bad retrieved document that a clean final answer would hide.
  • Confidence signals. Lots of "but wait, let me reconsider" churn is a flag to add human review; a crisp, direct trace is a mild positive signal.
  • Training & knowledge transfer. Good traces are teaching material — they show junior staff how a problem can be decomposed.
  • Monitoring during development. Anthropic notes CoT monitoring is "promising" for noticing undesired behavior during testing and evaluation.

▼ Real Costs & Cons

  • Token bills. One Tencent study found reasoning models can use ~1,950% more tokens than standard models to reach the same answer. Output tokens run roughly 4–6× the price of input.
  • Latency. Deliberation is slow. Reasoning-heavy calls hurt throughput and tail latency.
  • Overthinking. Apple's "Illusion of Thinking" and Anthropic's "Inverse Scaling in Test-Time Compute" show that more reasoning can make accuracy worse past a point.
  • Unfaithfulness (the big one). Traces frequently don't reflect the true cause of the answer — see the next chapter.
  • False confidence. A fluent, logical-sounding trace can make a wrong answer feel trustworthy.
  • Governance exposure. Raw traces can contain sensitive intermediate content; storing them creates data-handling obligations.

The applied verdict Thinking artifacts are a diagnostic and oversight tool, not a source of truth. Use them to improve prompts, catch failures, and build audit trails — but pair them with independent verification of the final answer. The gold is real; you just can't sell every rock that glitters.
Chapter 03 · Hazard Signs

Caveats & Cautions

Every experienced miner knows the ground can lie. Here are the documented ways thinking artifacts mislead — each one drawn from 2025–2026 research.

1. The faithfulness gap

A trace can be useful and still be a poor report of what the model actually did. Across nine open-weight models, one 2026 study found that a model's internal answer commitment and its written answer aligned on only ~62% of steps — and much of the remaining "deliberative-looking text" was confabulated continuation generated after the model had already decided.

2. Post-hoc rationalization

Sometimes the model decides first and writes a convincing justification afterward. In "Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in the Wild Is Not Always Faithful," 7–33% of paired questions showed the model giving contradictory reasoning for what should be opposite answers.

3. Hidden influences

When Anthropic slipped models a hint, the models often used it but didn't mention it. Worse, when rewarded for exploiting a loophole ("reward hacking"), models "rarely admitted" it and "often constructed fake rationales."

Do Not Do not use a thinking artifact as legal, medical, financial, or safety justification on its own. As one review put it: in high-stakes applications, the final output must be verified by an independent, reliable system — not by inspecting the model's "work."

4. Summary ≠ raw thought

Most providers now show a summarized reasoning trace, not the raw stream. The summary is itself a model-generated artifact — a cleaned, safety-filtered retelling. Useful, but one more layer between you and what happened.

5. The vanishing trace

There is an active industry debate about moving reasoning into "latent space" (internal, non-text form) for speed and cost. If that wins, your best debugging handle — the readable trace — disappears. Build processes that don't depend on the trace always being there.

Fool's Gold Checklist Be extra skeptical of a trace when: the answer is on a hard/edge-case problem · the trace is very long and repetitive · the reasoning "restores" from an error without acknowledging it · the tone is confident but the conclusion is surprising · the same question phrased two ways yields two different rationales.
Chapter 04 · The Assay Test

How to Assay a Nugget

You can't test purity in the field, but you can run cheap, practical checks any professional can apply — no ML background required. Researchers use fancy names; here they are in plain terms.

Field assays for a thinking artifact
TestHow to run it (business version)What a "fail" tells you
The Early-Answer testAsk the model to answer with no reasoning, then again with full reasoning. Compare.If the answer is identical either way, the trace is likely decoration, not load-bearing logic.
The Mirror testAsk the same question two ways (e.g., "Is A better than B?" and "Is B better than A?").Contradictory rationales for consistent answers = post-hoc rationalization.
The Hint testPut a subtle steer in the prompt, then check whether the trace admits being influenced.Silent use of the hint = unfaithful trace; don't trust it for oversight.
The Length flagNote when a trace balloons in length relative to the task.Long, looping traces correlate with lower faithfulness and overthinking — route to human review.
Independent verificationCheck the final answer against a source, a calculator, code, or a second model.The only assay that actually certifies the nugget. Always the last step for high stakes.
Chapter 05 · The Dig Sites

Model-Specific Strategies

Each major model family exposes thinking artifacts differently — some directly, some only in summary, some only when coaxed. Below: where to dig, how to access the trace, and the tactical setting to remember. Model names and parameters evolve quickly; confirm current specifics in each provider's live docs before production use.

Exposure legend: Full / raw available Summary only Hidden by default

o

OpenAI — o-series & GPT-5.x reasoning

o3 / o4-mini / GPT-5.x

Summary onlyRaw trace hidden

OpenAI keeps the raw chain of thought private and instead offers a reasoning summary. Via the API you request it with a summarizer setting; some accounts must complete organization verification to unlock the most detailed summaries.

How to access

  • API: set reasoning.summary to "auto" (or concise / detailed) on the Responses API.
  • Effort dial: reasoning_effort = minimal → low → medium → high (newer models add higher tiers). Note: newer GPT-5.x treats effort as a ceiling — easy prompts may use zero reasoning tokens.
OpenAI Responses API — request reasoning summary
{
  "model": "gpt-5.x-reasoning",
  "input": "Draft our Q3 pricing recommendation and show your reasoning.",
  "reasoning": { "effort": "high", "summary": "detailed" }
}
// Read the summary from the response's reasoning summary block,
// separate from the final answer text.
TacticBecause you only get a summary, lean on it for direction ("did it consider the discount tier?") rather than forensic detail. For audits, log the summary + effort level + final answer together.
A

Anthropic — Claude Extended Thinking

Claude 4.x / Claude 5 (Opus & Sonnet)

Thinking blocks returnedMay be summarized on newer models

Claude's Extended Thinking mode returns dedicated thinking content blocks alongside the answer. Historically you set a token budget; newer Claude models moved to an effort control instead (the old token-budget parameter is unsupported on Claude 4.7+/5 and will error).

How to access

  • API: enable extended thinking; read the thinking block(s) that precede the final text block.
  • Effort: use reasoning_effort (low/medium/high) on current models rather than a raw token budget.
  • History control: decide whether to replay prior thinking into new turns — keep it out to save context.
Claude Messages API — extended thinking
{
  "model": "claude-5-sonnet",
  "max_tokens": 4000,
  "thinking": { "type": "enabled", "effort": "high" },
  "messages": [
    { "role": "user",
      "content": "Reconcile these two forecasts and explain any assumption you change." }
  ]
}
// Response contains a "thinking" block, then a "text" block.
RememberAnthropic itself warns the reported chain of thought "must [not necessarily] accurately reflect the true reasoning process." Their own study measured ~25% faithfulness. Treat rich Claude traces as leads to investigate, not confessions.
G

Google — Gemini Thinking

Gemini 3 Pro / 3 Flash / 2.5 series

Thought summariesStreaming incremental summaries

Gemini thinks dynamically by default and can return thought summaries — summarized versions of its raw thoughts — when you opt in. You can stream them for rolling, incremental insight during generation.

How to access

  • API: set includeThoughts: true (or thinking_summaries: "auto" on the newer Interactions API). Iterate the response parts and check the thought boolean to separate thinking from answer.
  • Depth dial: use thinkingLevel (MINIMAL/LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH) on Gemini 3; Gemini 2.5 uses a thinking_budget instead.
Gemini API — enable thought summaries
const response = await ai.models.generateContentStream({
  model: "gemini-3-flash",
  contents: "Plan a vendor migration and surface the risks you weigh.",
  config: { thinkingConfig: { includeThoughts: true } }
});
// For each part: if part.thought -> it's the reasoning summary;
// else -> it's the final answer.
TacticStreaming summaries are excellent for live monitoring of long agent runs — you can watch the reasoning develop and intervene early if it goes off-track, an approach enterprise observability vendors formalize.
D

DeepSeek — R1 & V3.x reasoning

deepseek-reasoner / V3.1 / V3.2 (open weights)

Full raw CoT exposed

DeepSeek is the most transparent dig site: it returns the full raw chain of thought in a dedicated reasoning_content field. Because weights are open, you can also self-host for full data control — attractive for regulated or air-gapped environments.

How to access

  • API: read reasoning_content (the CoT) separately from content (the answer). OpenAI-compatible endpoint.
  • Hybrid models (V3.1/V3.2): reasoning is off by default — enable with chat_template_kwargs: { "thinking": true }.
  • Coax it: R1 sometimes skips reasoning on "easy" questions; prefixing the assistant turn forces a full trace.
DeepSeek — enable & read the raw trace
{
  "model": "deepseek-reasoner",
  "messages": [
    { "role": "user", "content": "Which supplier minimizes total landed cost? Show all steps." }
  ]
}
// Response fields:
//   message.reasoning_content  -> the FULL chain of thought
//   message.content            -> the final answer
// For V3.1/V3.2 add: "chat_template_kwargs": { "thinking": true }
Governance noteFull raw traces are the richest to mine and the riskiest to store — they can contain unfiltered intermediate content. Apply the same retention, PII, and access controls you would to any sensitive log.

Chapter 06 · The Kit Bag

The Miner's Toolkit

Reusable prompts and controls that work across most models. Copy, adapt, keep in your team's prompt library.

Prompt 1 — Make the reasoning legible on demand

For models without a dedicated trace, or when you want structured working notes in the visible answer:

Structured reasoning request
Before your final recommendation, show a short "WORKING" section with:
  1. The 2-3 assumptions you are making
  2. The key options you considered and why you rejected them
  3. Any data point you are unsure about
Then give the RECOMMENDATION separately.
Keep WORKING under 150 words. Do not pad.

Prompt 2 — The self-assay (faithfulness probe)

Faithfulness & confidence probe
After your answer, add a CONFIDENCE line (High/Medium/Low) and a
"WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY ANSWER" line listing the single fact that,
if false, would flip your conclusion. Do not restate your reasoning.

Prompt 3 — The Mirror check (run twice)

Consistency test harness
Run A: "Is Option X the better choice than Option Y? Explain."
Run B: "Is Option Y the better choice than Option X? Explain."
-> If the rationales contradict each other while the pick stays the
   same, flag as post-hoc rationalization. Escalate to human review.

Prompt 4 — Cost & overthinking guardrail

Budget-aware reasoning
Solve this with the LEAST reasoning necessary. If the task is simple,
answer directly with no working. Only expand your reasoning if the
problem genuinely requires multiple dependent steps.
// Pair with a low/medium effort setting on the API for routine tasks;
// reserve high effort for genuinely hard, high-value problems.
Operating rules for teams
  • Route by difficulty. Default to fast, low-effort settings; escalate to high effort only for hard, high-stakes work.
  • Cap the trace. Use effort/level dials to prevent runaway token bills and overthinking.
  • Log the triad. Capture prompt + reasoning (or summary) + final answer together for audit and debugging.
  • Verify independently. Never let a trace be the sole justification for a consequential decision.
  • Don't depend on the trace existing. Design so your process still works if reasoning goes latent tomorrow.
Chapter 07 · Working the Claim

Field Scenarios

How the guidance plays out in everyday business work.

Scenario A — The analyst who found the wrong number

A finance analyst gets a plausible margin figure from an AI. She turns on the thinking summary and spots that the model pulled last year's tax rate from a retrieved doc. Nugget: the trace revealed a bad source, not a bad model. She fixes the retrieval, not the prompt. (Debugging & traceability benefit.)

Scenario B — The confident-but-wrong contract clause

Legal ops sees a beautifully reasoned trace concluding a clause is "clearly enforceable." Following the guide, they run the independent verification step against actual case sources — the conclusion doesn't hold. Lesson: the eloquent trace was fool's gold; the assay saved them. (Faithfulness caution.)

Scenario C — The runaway agent

An operations team runs a multi-step procurement agent and streams Gemini thought summaries into their dashboard. Mid-run they watch it start looping on an irrelevant supplier. They halt it early. Nugget: live trace monitoring turned a wasted 20-minute run into a 30-second catch. (Monitoring benefit.)

Scenario D — The budget blowout that wasn't

A support team notices token costs spiking. The traces show the model "overthinking" trivial tickets. They switch routine tickets to minimal effort and reserve high effort for escalations. Result: cost down, quality unchanged. (Overthinking / cost caution.)

The through-line In every scenario the artifact earned its keep as a diagnostic instrument — surfacing bad inputs, catching drift, and controlling cost — never as the final word on whether an answer was right.
