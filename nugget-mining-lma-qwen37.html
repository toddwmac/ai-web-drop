The Search for AI Nuggets

A field guide for thinking artifact miners.

Modern LLMs don't just answer — they reason out loud. Those hidden thinking traces are a goldmine for business users who know how to dig. This guide shows you where to find them, how to read them, and when to trust them.

Chapter One

What Are Thinking Artifacts?

When you ask an LLM a question in 2026, you're not just getting an answer — you're getting the aftermath of a reasoning process. For the newest generation of "thinking" models (OpenAI's o-series, Claude's extended thinking, DeepSeek R1, Gemini's thinking mode, QwQ, Kimi K1.5, and others), that process is sometimes visible to you. The visible scraps of that process — the scratchpad, the backtracking, the self-corrections, the plan sketches — are what we call thinking artifacts.

In plain terms
A thinking artifact is the LLM's "rough draft" — the internal monologue it generates before (or alongside) the final answer you see. Some models show it to you. Some hide it. Some only show it if you know how to ask.

Where do they come from?

The idea traces back to Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, introduced by Wei et al. at Google in 2022: asking a model to "think step by step" dramatically improved performance on math, logic, and reasoning. By 2024, OpenAI's o1 was the first widely-available model to do this thinking automatically — generating thousands of hidden tokens of reasoning before responding. Anthropic, DeepSeek, Google, Alibaba, and xAI followed with their own variants through 2025 and 2026.

Three flavors you'll encounter

● Direct Export
Visible by default

Models like DeepSeek R1 and QwQ print their <think>…</think> block right in the answer. You see the raw reasoning without doing anything.

● Prompted / Toggled
Visible on request

ChatGPT's "Show thinking", Claude's extended-thinking toggle, Gemini's thinking panel, and API flags. You have to turn it on or ask for it.

● Hidden / Distilled
Not available

Many smaller or "distilled" models were trained on thinking traces but don't expose them. You can sometimes coax out reasoning with clever prompts — but you're seeing a reconstruction, not the real thing.

Why should a business user care?

Because the thinking trace is often more valuable than the answer. The answer tells you what. The thinking trace tells you:

  • ✦ Why the model chose that answer (traceability)
  • ✦ What it's uncertain about (honest confidence signals)
  • ✦ Where it got stuck or self-corrected (quality signal)
  • ✦ What alternatives it considered and rejected (exploration depth)
  • ✦ Whether it actually read your source documents (grounding audit)

For a procurement manager, that's the difference between "here's a vendor recommendation" and "here's the reasoning behind this vendor recommendation — including the parts I'm least sure about." That's an AI nugget.

Chapter Two

What the Research Says

A plain-language summary of 2024–2026 findings.

The Case FOR Mining

Pros
✓ Accuracy jumps on complex tasks

OpenAI reported o1 solving 83% of International Math Olympiad qualifying problems vs 13% for GPT-4o. The "thinking" step is the driver. Business analogue: multi-step analyses (pricing, forecasting, contract review) show measurable accuracy gains.

✓ Better calibration of uncertainty

Research from Anthropic (2025) showed thinking models can express honest uncertainty ("I'm about 60% sure") more faithfully than standard models, which tend to sound equally confident whether right or wrong.

✓ Self-correction becomes visible

Google's Gemini technical reports note that thinking traces surface when a model catches its own mistakes mid-reasoning — critical for audit trails in regulated industries.

✓ Built-in rationale for decisions

For enterprise workflows (approvals, recommendations, risk flags), the thinking trace is a first-draft justification you can keep, edit, or discard — saving hours of "explain your answer" follow-ups.

✓ Training signal for your team

Reading how an LLM structures reasoning is surprisingly useful as a prompt-engineering and analyst-training tool. You learn what "good reasoning" looks like on your domain data.

✓ Distillation goldmine (with caution)

DeepSeek's R1 release famously showed that smaller models trained on high-quality thinking traces from larger models can punch far above their weight — a major cost lever for enterprise deployments.

⚠️

The Case AGAINST Trusting Them Blindly

Cons
✗ Post-hoc rationalization

Turing Award winner Yoshua Bengio and others (2025) warn that thinking traces can be plausible-sounding justifications generated after the fact, not the true causal chain of the answer. A confident-sounding trace ≠ honest reasoning.

✗ Token cost explosion

Thinking traces routinely add 2,000–20,000+ tokens per query. At enterprise scale, this can 5–10x your inference bill. Apple's 2025 paper "The Illusion of Thinking" argued that for many problems, you're paying for reasoning theater.

✗ Latency tax

A 10-second thinking pass is fine for strategic analysis. It's a dealbreaker for customer-facing chat. Latency-sensitive workflows need thinking budgets, not thinking on everything.

✗ "Reasoning-washing"

Several 2025 studies (including work from Stanford HAI) found that simply displaying a thinking panel makes users over-trust the output — even when the trace is shallow or fabricated. The UI itself is a persuasion vector.

✗ PII and policy leakage

Thinking traces sometimes surface content the final answer correctly redacts — including copyrighted text, system prompts, or sensitive retrieved documents. This is a real compliance risk.

✗ Inconsistent availability

Providers toggle thinking visibility on and off across versions. A workflow built on reading today's traces may break tomorrow. Never architect a critical pipeline around a thinking artifact you don't control.

The Balanced View: When It's Worth It

Verdict

The research converges on a pragmatic position: thinking artifacts are most valuable for high-stakes, low-frequency, complex-reasoning work — and least valuable (or actively harmful) for high-frequency, simple-Q&A work. The sweet spot for business users:

🎯 Strategic analysis
Market sizing, competitive moves, M&A rationale
📊 Multi-step data work
Financial models, cohort analysis, anomaly triage
📜 Document reasoning
Contract review, policy interpretation, compliance
🔧 Debug & troubleshooting
Root cause analysis, engineering triage
✍️ Writing with intent
Messaging strategy, stakeholder comms, negotiations
🧪 Hypothesis generation
Research, product ideas, experiment design

Chapter Three

Caveats & Cautions

Read this before you trust anything you mine.

⚠️ Caveat 1

The trace is not the thought.

LLMs don't "think" the way humans do. The thinking trace is a generated text sequence that the model learned improves its final answer. Treat it as a useful scaffold, not a window into cognition.

⚠️ Caveat 2

Fluency ≠ fidelity.

A beautifully written thinking trace can be completely disconnected from how the answer was actually produced. Smooth prose in the trace is a style, not a guarantee of honesty.

⚠️ Caveat 3

Traces can leak what answers won't.

Sensitive documents, system instructions, and even competitor data sometimes surface in thinking traces while being correctly filtered from the final answer. Always sanitize thinking traces before sharing externally.

⚠️ Caveat 4

Cost & latency are real constraints.

A single "thinking" query can cost 10–50× a standard query. Budget it like you'd budget analyst time — spend it where the stakes justify it.

⚠️ Caveat 5

Provider control means instability.

You do not own the thinking artifact. Providers can (and do) change format, length, and availability without notice. Don't build fragile automations on a specific trace structure.

⚠️ Caveat 6

Over-trust is the biggest risk.

Research (Stanford HAI, 2025) shows that showing thinking traces increases user trust regardless of trace quality. The fix: build habits of spot-checking the trace against the answer and the source.

The Golden Rule of Nugget Mining

A thinking artifact is a lead, not a verdict. Use it to direct your attention, then verify the claim against the original source. The best miners treat every nugget as "probable until confirmed."

Chapter Four

The Model Field Guide

Model-by-model tactics for finding and reading thinking artifacts.

Information current as of early 2026. Always verify availability in your specific tier and region.

🟤

Claude (Anthropic)

Claude Sonnet 4.5 · Claude Opus 4.5 · Claude Haiku 4.5
● Prompted / Toggled

Where thinking lives

  • ✦ claude.ai: "Extended Thinking" toggle in the model dropdown. Once on, Claude shows a collapsible thinking panel above each response.
  • ✦ API: thinking: { type: "enabled", budget_tokens: N }. Returned as separate thinking content blocks alongside text blocks.
  • ✦ Claude Code: Thinking is on by default for agentic tasks; streamed in a dedicated pane.

What makes Claude's thinking distinctive

Claude's traces are typically well-structured, self-questioning, and explicit about uncertainties. They often include numbered reasoning steps, explicit "let me re-check" moments, and a clear handoff to the final answer. Anthropic has invested heavily in making these traces auditable rather than just long.

Tactical prompt to elicit richer thinking

# Use with Extended Thinking ON, budget_tokens ≥ 4000
Before answering, walk through:
1. What exactly am I being asked to decide?
2. What information do I have, and what's missing?
3. What are the top 3 alternatives I considered?
4. Which assumption in my reasoning is weakest?
5. What would change my mind?

Then give me the answer with confidence.

Business use-case sweet spot

Policy interpretation, stakeholder comms, contract red-flag review, structured decision memos. Claude's thinking shines on tasks where nuance and tradeoffs matter more than raw calculation.

✦

ChatGPT / OpenAI o-series

o1 · o3 · o3-mini · o4-mini · GPT-5 with thinking
● Toggled

Where thinking lives

  • ✦ chatgpt.com: "Show thinking" toggle on o-series and GPT-5-thinking responses. Renders as an expandable "Thought for N seconds" block.
  • ✦ API: The reasoning content is not directly exposed via the Responses API as of 2026 — a notable limitation for enterprise pipelines. You can only see the final answer plus a reasoning_effort setting.
  • ✦ Reasoning effort: low / medium / high / xhigh controls how long the model thinks.

What makes o-series thinking distinctive

o-series traces tend to be denser and more mathematical than Claude's. They're optimized for problems where exhaustive exploration and self-verification help — math, code, logic puzzles, and structured data analysis. Less conversational, more "show your work."

Tactical prompt for better traces

# Pair with reasoning_effort: "high"
Analyze the attached quarterly data.

Show your work explicitly:
- State each assumption you're making
- Do the math step-by-step (don't skip intermediate numbers)
- Flag any figures that look anomalous and why
- End with a one-line verdict and your confidence (0–100%)

Business use-case sweet spot

Financial modeling, SQL/code generation, spreadsheet analysis, quantitative research, operations planning. Pick o-series when you want rigor over rhetoric.

🔷

DeepSeek R1 / R1-0528

Open-weight reasoning model, self-hosted options
● Direct Export

Where thinking lives

  • ✦ Everywhere, by default: R1 emits its reasoning between literal <think>…</think> XML tags as part of the completion. No toggle needed.
  • ✦ API: The same tags appear in the content field. You can parse them out or leave them in.
  • ✦ Self-hosted: Because the weights are open, you can run R1 on your own infra and keep thinking traces fully internal — a real enterprise advantage.

What makes DeepSeek's thinking distinctive

R1's traces are very long, highly introspective, and occasionally bilingual (Chinese/English code-switching in early versions). They show genuine backtracking — "wait, that can't be right" moments — which is rare and valuable for auditability. The "aha" moments are often visible.

Tactical prompt

# R1 thinks automatically; steer the quality with structure
Task: Review this customer churn dataset and identify the top 3
predictive factors.

In your <think> block, please:
- Consider at least 5 candidate factors before narrowing to 3
- Explicitly state why each rejected factor was dropped
- Show the correlation reasoning (even approximate)

Final answer: Ranked list with brief rationale.

Business use-case sweet spot

Data analysis, research synthesis, code review, cost-sensitive deployments (R1 is dramatically cheaper than frontier closed models). Ideal when you want transparent reasoning on your own infrastructure.

◆

Gemini (Google)

Gemini 2.5 Pro · 2.5 Flash · 3 Pro (thinking mode)
● Toggled

Where thinking lives

  • ✦ Gemini app: Thinking mode exposes a "Thinking" panel with the reasoning trace.
  • ✦ API: thinkingConfig: { thinkingBudget: N }. Returned as a THOUGHT part alongside the response.
  • ✦ Dynamic thinking: Gemini can also auto-scale thinking tokens based on problem complexity.

What makes Gemini's thinking distinctive

Gemini traces are strong on multimodal reasoning — they'll walk through an image, a PDF, a code snippet, and a chart in a single coherent chain. The 2.5 Pro "1M token context" + thinking combo is particularly useful for long-document analysis.

Tactical prompt

# Works great with Gemini's long context
Using the attached 40-page vendor proposal:

Think through (show your reasoning):
1. What is the vendor actually committing to?
2. What are the unstated risks or ambiguities?
3. How does pricing compare to the 3 benchmarks I attached?
4. What questions should we ask before signing?

Cite page numbers for every claim.

Business use-case sweet spot

Long-document review (contracts, RFPs, reports), multimodal analysis (slides + data + images), research synthesis across many sources.

Q

QwQ / Qwen (Alibaba)

QwQ-32B · Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking
● Direct Export

Where thinking lives

  • ✦ Like DeepSeek R1, QwQ emits <think>…</think> tags directly in the completion.
  • ✦ Qwen3's "thinking mode" toggle in the official chat app; in the API, controlled by enable_thinking: true.
  • ✦ Hybrid mode (Qwen3): You can mix thinking and non-thinking turns in the same conversation — useful for cost control.

What makes QwQ/Qwen's thinking distinctive

QwQ traces are concise but methodical, often with explicit self-verification steps ("Let me verify: ..."). The 32B version punches well above its weight on math and code for the price. Qwen3's hybrid approach is genuinely novel for enterprise cost management.

Tactical prompt

# Use for technical problem-solving
Problem: Our API latency spiked 4x overnight after the deploy.

In your thinking, systematically:
- Enumerate likely root causes (at least 6)
- For each, name the diagnostic query that would confirm/rule out
- Rank by probability based on the logs I've shared
- Propose the first 3 actions for the on-call engineer

Business use-case sweet spot

Engineering troubleshooting, math-heavy analysis, cost-conscious reasoning workloads, on-prem / private deployments.

⚡

Grok (xAI)

Grok 3 · Grok 4 with Think mode
● Toggled

Where thinking lives

  • ✦ grok.com / X: "Think" mode toggle; reasoning appears in a collapsible block.
  • ✦ API: reasoning_effort parameter on supported endpoints.
  • ✦ Think / Big Brain: Two tiers — "Think" for standard reasoning, "Big Brain" for deep multi-step exploration (longer, costlier).

What makes Grok's thinking distinctive

Grok traces tend to be more conversational and less structured than o-series. They integrate real-time X/web context when available, which makes them useful for fast-moving market intel — but also harder to audit.

Tactical prompt

# Good for market / competitive intel
Think mode ON.

Our competitor just announced [X]. Walk through:
1. What this likely means strategically (3 interpretations)
2. Which interpretation is most probable and why
3. What signal would confirm each one in the next 30 days
4. What our response options are, ranked by risk/reward

Business use-case sweet spot

Competitive intelligence, real-time market monitoring, brainstorming, exploratory research where speed + breadth matter more than rigorous verification.

Chapter Five

Tactical Prompts

Copy, paste, adapt. These work across most thinking-enabled models.

P1

The "Show Your Reasoning" Primering

Use when you want richer thinking traces from any model
Before you answer, I want to see how you're thinking, not just what
you conclude. In your reasoning trace, please:

1. Restate the question in your own words to confirm understanding.
2. List the information you have and the information you'd ideally want.
3. Identify 2–3 possible approaches, and briefly say why you're choosing one.
4. Walk through your chosen approach step by step, showing intermediate results.
5. Flag any assumptions you're making and any points of uncertainty.
6. If you catch yourself mid-reasoning, say so out loud.

Then give your final answer, clearly separated.

💡 When to use: At the start of a complex analytical conversation. Sets a reasoning-first tone for the whole thread.

P2

The "Red Team Your Own Answer"

Stress-test a conclusion before you act on it
Here's my proposed decision: [paste your decision/recommendation]

Think through it like a skeptical advisor:
- What are the strongest arguments against this decision?
- Which assumption, if wrong, would most damage the outcome?
- What would a smart person who disagrees point to?
- What's the worst realistic outcome if I proceed?
- What cheap check could I run in the next 48 hours to de-risk it?

Then give me a revised confidence level (0–100) and a one-line recommendation.

💡 When to use: Before committing to any significant business decision. The thinking trace this produces is often more valuable than the final verdict.

P3

The "Grounding Audit"

Force the model to show what it actually read
Using ONLY the documents I've attached, answer: [your question]

In your thinking, for every claim in your final answer:
- Quote the exact sentence from the source that supports it
- Name the document and page/section
- If no direct support exists, say "NOT GROUNDED" and explain your inference

Final answer should be clearly separated and flag any ungrounded claims inline.

💡 When to use: Contract review, compliance analysis, any task where "did the model actually read this?" is a real concern.

P4

The "Confidence Ladder"

Extract honest uncertainty instead of polished certainty
Answer the following, but calibrate honestly:

Question: [your question]

In your thinking:
- Rate your confidence on each sub-claim from 0–100
- Flag anything below 70% as "LOW CONFIDENCE"
- State what evidence would raise or lower your confidence

Final answer format:
- Headline answer
- Confidence: X%
- What I'm most sure about:
- What I'm least sure about:
- What would change my mind:

💡 When to use: Whenever you'll act on the answer. Calibrated confidence is arguably the single most valuable output a thinking model can give a decision-maker.

P5

The "Thinking Trace Miner" (for non-thinking models)

When the model doesn't natively expose thinking — coax it out
# Works on most standard LLMs without native thinking mode

I want you to think out loud before answering. Put all your reasoning
inside <thinking> ... </thinking> tags, then give the final answer
afterwards.

Inside the thinking block:
- Consider the problem from multiple angles
- Work through the logic step by step
- Note any doubts or alternative interpretations
- Self-correct if you catch an error

The question is: [your question]
⚠️ Important distinction

This produces simulated thinking — a reconstruction, not the model's actual internal reasoning. It's still useful (often improves answer quality), but don't treat it with the same trust as a native thinking trace from an o-series or R1 model.

Chapter Six

The ASSAY Framework

How to tell a real nugget from fool's gold.

Just like a gold prospector assays a sample before declaring a strike, use this five-point checklist on any thinking trace you plan to act on.

A
Anchored

Does the trace cite specific facts, numbers, or sources — or does it reason in vague generalities?

S
Self-correcting

Does the trace show the model catching its own mistakes, or is it a straight line to the answer?

S
Specific

Does it give concrete intermediate results (numbers, names, steps) or hand-wave?

A
Alternative-aware

Does it consider paths not taken and explain why they were rejected?

Y
Yield-clear

Does it honestly flag uncertainty, or does it end with false confidence?

Scoring a Thinking Trace

✓ A real nugget looks like this:
The user asked for Q3 revenue by region. Looking at the CSV: EMEA is in €, not $ — I need to convert at 1.08. Wait, let me check the date on the exchange rate… it's Q2-end rate. [Self-correction] Using Q3 avg rate of 1.07 instead. Two approaches: (a) convert each row, (b) sum then convert. Will do (a) for auditability. NA=$18.2M, EMEA=€12.1M × 1.07 = $12.9M, APAC=$11.2M. Total = $42.3M. Confidence: ~90% — main uncertainty is FX rate choice.
ASSAY score: 5/5. Anchored (CSV data), Self-correcting (FX rate), Specific (exact numbers), Alternative-aware (two methods), Yield-clear (90% confidence stated).
✗ Fool's gold looks like this:
The user wants to know Q3 revenue by region. I should look at the data carefully and analyze it. Revenue is an important business metric that companies track quarterly. Let me think about the regions mentioned in the data. After careful analysis, I will provide the breakdown the user needs. I'm confident in my analysis of this data.
ASSAY score: 0/5. No anchoring, no self-correction, no specifics, no alternatives, false confidence. This is reasoning theater — looks like thinking, but adds nothing.

Chapter Seven

Business Examples

What this looks like in real roles, on real days.

Scenario 1

Procurement Manager — Vendor Selection

The task: Pick between 3 finalist vendors for a new CRM.

Without thinking artifacts: You get a 2-page recommendation. Sounds good. You have no idea why the model preferred Vendor B over A.

With thinking artifacts (Claude extended thinking): You see the model explicitly weighing pricing tiers against your team size, noting that Vendor A's "unlimited seats" only applies above 500 users (you have 280), catching that Vendor C's "99.99% uptime" SLA excludes weekends, and flagging that its confidence in Vendor B is ~75% because of limited data on their EU data residency.

The nugget: You realize the recommendation was nearly wrong because of a pricing-tier nuance the model caught in its own trace. You go back to Vendor A with a negotiated tier instead.

Scenario 2

Finance Analyst — Quarterly Variance Review

The task: Explain why OpEx came in 12% over budget.

With thinking artifacts (ChatGPT o4-mini, high effort): The trace shows the model methodically testing 5 hypotheses (FX, one-time accruals, hiring timing, vendor price increases, classification errors), explicitly ruling out 3 with intermediate math, and landing on two co-occurring drivers with confidence 65% and 80%.

The nugget: The thinking trace becomes the outline of your variance memo — saving an hour of structuring and, more importantly, capturing the alternatives you'd otherwise forget to mention.

Scenario 3

Engineering Lead — Incident Post-Mortem

The task: Root-cause a 45-minute production outage.

With thinking artifacts (DeepSeek R1, self-hosted): The trace walks through the log timeline, notices a 3-second gap that doesn't match the hypothesis, backtracks, and proposes an alternative cause involving a cache invalidation race condition. It explicitly flags what telemetry is missing to confirm.

The nugget: Your team gets a structured investigation path in 30 seconds that would have taken 30 minutes to assemble. The "missing telemetry" callout becomes a real action item.

Scenario 4

Marketing Director — Campaign Positioning

The task: Position a new B2B product against a dominant incumbent.

With thinking artifacts (Grok Think + Claude): Grok surfaces current market chatter and competitor messaging; Claude's thinking trace then walks through three positioning frames, explicitly weighing each against your customer research, and flags that the "cheaper" frame contradicts your brand equity.

The nugget: You avoid a positioning mistake you wouldn't have caught from a polished final recommendation. The "rejected alternatives" section is worth more than the final answer.

Scenario 5

Legal / Compliance — Contract Red-Flag Review

The task: Flag risks in a 30-page vendor MSA.

With thinking artifacts (Gemini 2.5 Pro, thinking on): The trace walks through the document section by section, citing page numbers, flagging that Section 11.3's indemnity clause is uncapped, that the auto-renewal notice window is 15 days (industry standard is 60), and that the data processing addendum references a version of GDPR guidance that's been superseded.

The nugget: Every flag in the thinking trace comes with the exact clause that triggered it — making your review a verification pass, not a discovery pass.

Chapter Eight

Conclusion: Mine Smart

The next era of AI literacy isn't about asking better questions — it's about reading the reasoning and knowing what to trust.

Thinking artifacts are the most significant new surface area LLMs have exposed to business users since chatbots arrived. For the first time, you can see not just what an AI recommends but how it got there — and that's transformative for any workflow where traceability, auditability, and trust matter.

But a thinking trace is not an oracle. It's a reasoning artifact — a rich, imperfect, often-useful byproduct of how modern models generate better answers. Used well, it's a force multiplier. Used naively, it's a persuasion vector that makes you feel more confident than you should be.

The miners who'll come out ahead in the next two years are the ones who develop two complementary habits:

  1. Know where the traces live — per model, per interface, per API. This guide is your map.
  2. Assay before you act — apply the ASSAY framework to every trace you plan to base a decision on.
  3. Budget thinking like analyst time — spend it where the stakes justify it; skip it where they don't.
  4. Sanitize before you share — thinking traces can leak what final answers correctly hide.
  5. Stay skeptical of your own trust — the trace panel is designed to feel reassuring. Verify anyway.

The gold is real. So is fool's gold. The skill — the only skill that ultimately matters — is telling them apart.
