Executive recommendation

Bottom line: For a 5,000–6,000 lb enclosed trailer behind the 2026 Forest River Solera 22N4X4 in the Rockies, the Equal-i-zer 4-Point Sway Control is the more conservative default choice if the Solera receiver is confirmed to be weight-distribution rated. It offers stronger, more adjustable front-axle load restoration and a long record of controlling sway on heavy, broadside trailers. The tradeoff is noise, mass, grease, and a harsher feel.

The Andersen No-Sway remains attractive if the owner prioritizes a quiet, clean, lighter, smoother system and if scale weights show the Solera’s front axle, rear axle, and receiver loads are all within specification with Andersen’s lower weight-transfer behavior. It is less compelling when front-axle restoration is critical, when tongue weight approaches the high end of the estimate, or when trailer coupler / A-frame geometry is unusual.

Recommended sizing for this scenario

  • Expected loaded tongue weight: 500–900 lb using the common 10–15% bumper-pull guideline for a 5,000–6,000 lb trailer. Enclosed trailers often need disciplined loading because broad side area and rear cargo placement can worsen sway.
  • Equal-i-zer: likely the 90-00-1000 kit if measured tongue weight stays between 600–1,000 lb; consider 90-00-1200 only if verified loaded tongue weight exceeds 1,000 lb or future trailer weight demands it. Do not overspring unnecessarily.
  • Andersen: choose by ball size, shank drop/rise, receiver size, and frame brackets. A 2-5/16 in ball / 2 in shank Andersen kit is commonly appropriate for 5k–6k enclosed cargo trailers, but the trailer coupler and frame must be compatible.
Non-negotiable: verify the actual receiver label on the Solera. Ford notes that a hitch label provides both weight-carrying and weight-distributing capacities; the installed RV receiver and Forest River frame extension determine whether WD equipment is allowed.

How the systems work

Equal-i-zer 4-Point Sway Control

Equal-i-zer uses traditional spring bars plus integrated friction. Manufacturer material describes four sway-control points: two friction points in the hitch head where the spring-arm sockets rotate, and two at the rigid L-brackets mounted to the trailer frame. The spring bars both distribute tongue weight and provide friction resistance to trailer yaw.

Manufacturer specifications list models from 400/4,000 lb through 1,600/16,000 lb. The 90-00-1000 model is rated for 1,000 lb maximum tongue weight and 10,000 lb maximum gross trailer weight; the 90-00-1200 is rated for 1,200/12,000 lb.

Andersen No-Sway / No-Bounce

Andersen replaces spring bars with tension chains, urethane springs, and a tapered friction-cone ball housing. Andersen says the ball turns with the trailer, the system is grease-free, and sway control self-adjusts as tongue load pushes the tapered ball into brake-like friction material.

Andersen lists kits up to 16,000 lb GTWR and 1,600 lb tongue weight depending on shank/receiver. Its help material states that with about 900 lb of tongue weight, users can expect roughly 30% of the weight to be transferred, leaving the rest on the rear of the tow vehicle.

Equal-i-zer — pros

  • Strong front-axle load restoration adjustability.
  • Integrated sway control with no separate friction bar.
  • Proven and widely supported by RV dealers.
  • Good fit for higher tongue weights in the 600–1,000 lb range.
  • Can be tuned using objective fender-height and scale data.

Equal-i-zer — cons

  • Noisy at low speed and in tight turns.
  • Heavy components; greasy head sockets.
  • Can create a stiffer, more abrupt ride on rough pavement.
  • Requires careful setup and correct torque.
  • May need accessory brackets if trailer frame is unusual or crowded.

Andersen — pros

  • Light, clean, and easy to store.
  • Quiet, grease-free ball/coupler movement.
  • Urethane springs damp bounce and chucking.
  • Users often praise smooth towing and simple routine hookup once dialed in.
  • Good option when tow vehicle has ample axle margin and only moderate weight transfer is needed.

Andersen — cons

  • Often transfers less weight to the front axle than spring-bar hitches.
  • Coupler geometry, underslung couplers, and frame-bracket movement can reduce performance.
  • Some couplers are specifically not compatible.
  • Less compelling near axle/receiver limits.
  • Friction cone and chains are unique wear/load paths that require inspection.

Rocky Mountain operating factors

Why the Rockies change the decision: the issue is not merely whether the hitch can tow 6,000 lb. It is whether the complete rig remains controllable during descending curves, gusts in passes, rough expansion joints, emergency braking, and when a semi passes a tall enclosed trailer.
  • Front axle authority: A WD hitch should restore enough front axle load for predictable steering. Ford’s current towing guidance recommends 50% front axle load restoration when using load bars.
  • Rear axle reserve: The Solera’s 9,600 lb rear GAWR must absorb passengers, gear, water, the motorhome body, rear storage, hitch hardware, and a 500–900 lb tongue load before WD transfer.
  • Enclosed trailer side area: Box trailers catch wind. Proper tongue weight and anti-sway damping matter more than with a low utility trailer.
  • Trailer brakes: A 5k–6k enclosed trailer needs well-adjusted electric brakes, a quality controller, breakaway battery, and tires rated for sustained highway heat.
  • Altitude and grades: The 7.3L gas V8 is strong, but naturally aspirated engines lose power at elevation. Descending control and brake temperature become the bigger safety concern.
  • 4x4 lift: The Solera 22N4X4’s lift and off-road tires may require more drop in the hitch shank to keep the trailer level or slightly nose-down.

Decision rule

If CAT-scale results show the Andersen restores adequate front axle load while keeping rear axle and receiver loads under rating, Andersen is a valid comfort-focused choice. If the front axle remains light, rear axle is close to rating, or trailer sway appears in wind, choose the Equal-i-zer and tune it carefully.

Setup checklist for this rig

  1. Confirm receiver label: record weight-carrying maximum trailer weight, weight-carrying tongue weight, weight-distributing trailer weight, and weight-distributing tongue weight.
  2. Confirm Forest River approval: RV frame extensions can be the limiting component even when the Ford chassis has high GCWR.
  3. Measure loaded tongue weight: target 10–15% of actual trailer weight; for 6,000 lb, that is 600–900 lb.
  4. Set trailer level: loaded trailer should be level or slightly nose-down, not nose-high.
  5. Scale the rig: record front axle, rear axle, trailer axles, and combined weight with WD off and on.
  6. Tune WD: aim for compliant front-axle restoration without exceeding rear GAWR, front GAWR, hitch rating, or trailer A-frame limits.
  7. Road test conservatively: start on local roads, then highway, then mild grades before high passes.
  8. Inspect after 50–100 miles: bracket movement, torque, chain/spring condition, hitch head bolts, coupler latch, trailer brake temperatures, and tire pressures.

Manufacturer data + user feedback themes

Equal-i-zer feedback themes

  • Users commonly report excellent sway control and confidence in wind and bad weather.
  • The most repeated complaint is noise: creaking, groaning, and popping during slow turns. Some users add bracket jackets/noise reducers.
  • Owners like that, once adjusted, connection/disconnection is repeatable and backing is allowed.
  • Some users dislike grease and the weight of spring bars and head assembly.

Andersen feedback themes

  • Users commonly praise the quiet, clean, light design and smooth ride.
  • Several forum reports praise sway control but criticize limited front-axle load restoration on heavier trailers.
  • Some owners with underslung couplers or unusual A-frame geometry report chain-angle problems, bracket movement, or the need for longer/welded brackets.
  • Reports on customer support are mixed: many praise Andersen support, while some older forum threads contain concerns about wear, coupler stress, and long-term reliability.
Interpretation: user feedback aligns with the engineering tradeoff. Equal-i-zer is the stronger mechanical weight-distribution tool; Andersen is the cleaner comfort tool with strong sway damping but potentially less load restoration.
